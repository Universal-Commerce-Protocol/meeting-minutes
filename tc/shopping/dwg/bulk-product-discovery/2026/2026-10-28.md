# UCP Bulk Product Discovery Domain Working Group (DWG) Meeting - 2026-10-28

- **Sponsoring DTC:** Shopping Tech Council
- **Charter:** [DWG Charter: Bulk Product Discovery](https://github.com/Universal-Commerce-Protocol/ucp/issues/682)
- **Led by:** Alex Jansen (co-chair)

> The group evaluated product specification recommendations and attribute coverage for the Bulk Product Discovery feed design, debated whether expanding product variant shapes falls within the DWG charter, and reviewed the target release timeline for the immovable December 10, 2026 release. Key decisions included excluding common variant attributes (such as color and size) and flat native checkout booleans from the UCP core specification in favor of existing options, metadata, and platform extensions; keeping brand, condition, and barcode descriptions under refined catalog definitions; and isolating delta feed discussions into a follow-up phase so the working group can review and sign off on Ilya Grigorik's base feed capability pull request (#866) on next week's call.

## 2. Technical Discussions & Working Group Consensus

- **Topic:** Scope of product specification changes in domain working group
  - **Discussion:** Alex Jansen and James Andersen reviewed the domain working group charter to verify whether proposing modifications to the core product variant schema falls within the group's remit. Alex Jansen noted the charter explicitly contemplates potential product schema adjustments, while James Andersen recalled guidance from Ilya Grigorik to synthesize findings within the domain working group before bringing recommendations to the Shopping Tech Council for formal adoption.
  - **Working Group Position:** Consensus to use the working group to identify schema discrepancies, common merchant requirements, and candidate additions across feed formats, surfacing recommendations to the Shopping Tech Council for approval.
  - **Context:** [DWG Charter: Bulk Product Discovery](https://github.com/Universal-Commerce-Protocol/ucp/issues/682)

- **Topic:** Evaluating brand attribute in product schema
  - **Discussion:** Alex Jansen reported high feed coverage for the `brand` attribute (product manufacturer or brand name) across Google and Meta feeds. Ilya Grigorik noted that while brand is a universal concept, logistically establishing provenance and verification is complex, raising questions on whether brand should be modeled as a string, a logo reference, or a structured cryptographic claim. Ilya Grigorik recommended keeping core catalog schemas focused while distinguishing feed-only properties from persistent product entities.
  - **Working Group Position:** Keep `brand` as a candidate attribute for further analysis, deferring top-level schema additions until its exact structural representation and verification requirements are resolved.
  - **Context:** [feat: [DRAFT] feed capability for bulk catalog access #866](https://github.com/Universal-Commerce-Protocol/ucp/pull/866)

- **Topic:** Distinguishing barcodes from manufacturer part numbers
  - **Discussion:** Alex Jansen highlighted high feed coverage for `condition` and argued that physical barcodes must remain distinct from manufacturer part numbers (MPNs). Barcodes represent industry-standard scanning identifiers—such as Global Trade Item Numbers (GTINs), Universal Product Codes (UPCs), and International Standard Book Numbers (ISBNs)—whereas MPNs are arbitrary, manufacturer-specific alphanumeric codes. Ilya Grigorik noted that the catalog schema already reserves an open object array for product identifiers, and Tejas Mehta confirmed that Shopify merchants store barcodes and MPNs in separate data models with distinct semantics. Vinod Kumar proposed updating the description of the `barcodes` field to resolve terminology contradictions without altering the underlying object schema.
  - **Working Group Position:** Retain `condition` in feeds, preserve the separation between industry-standard barcodes and manufacturer part numbers, and update the schema description for the `barcodes` field to clarify terminology.
  - **Context:** [feat: [DRAFT] feed capability for bulk catalog access #866](https://github.com/Universal-Commerce-Protocol/ucp/pull/866)

- **Topic:** Offer price effective dates and time-bounded pricing
  - **Discussion:** Alex Jansen and Vinod Kumar examined modeling promotional sale windows via start and end timestamps (`offer price effective date`). Alex Jansen identified a semantic conflict with Universal Commerce Protocol core schemas: because `price` is required while `list_price` specifies the optional strike-through comparison price, bounding `price` by time windows could leave a variant without a valid required price outside the promotional period. Ilya Grigorik clarified that `list_price` and `price` are orthogonal fields and that time-bounded effective pricing represents net-new additive semantics that behave differently in cached bulk feeds than in synchronous real-time lookups.
  - **Working Group Position:** No decision. The group agreed that the operational differences between real-time lookups and point-in-time feed snapshots require further design before altering price semantics.
  - **Context:** [feat: [DRAFT] feed capability for bulk catalog access #866](https://github.com/Universal-Commerce-Protocol/ucp/pull/866)

- **Topic:** Variant identifying properties and taxonomy mapping
  - **Discussion:** The group debated whether six common variant identifying attributes—`color`, `size`, `gender`, `age_group`, `material`, and `pattern`—should become top-level first-class attributes in Universal Commerce Protocol or remain modeled via options and metadata. Alex Jansen noted heavy usage in apparel feeds. Ilya Grigorik cautioned against "pork-barreling" proprietary platform taxonomies into the core protocol, advocating for open options and category mappings. Jason Mathnov described PayPal's distinction between selectable and descriptive variant options. Vinod Kumar suggested aligning metadata keys with declared category taxonomies. Tejas Mehta highlighted risks around disparate category structures and closed enumerations across platforms. Ilya Grigorik and James Andersen emphasized that Universal Commerce Protocol avoids closed enumerations, allowing platforms to compose field sets independently.
  - **Working Group Position:** Consensus not to make the six attributes first-class core properties in Universal Commerce Protocol. Platforms will map variant attributes into existing `options` and `categories`, the group will evaluate descriptive/displayable options, and platform extensions will define required field sets.
  - **Context:** [feat: [DRAFT] feed capability for bulk catalog access #866](https://github.com/Universal-Commerce-Protocol/ucp/pull/866)

- **Topic:** Native checkout boolean indicator in bulk feeds
  - **Discussion:** Vinod Kumar proposed an `is_native_checkout` flat boolean attribute at the variant level to indicate whether direct checkout is supported. Reid Kairalla and James Andersen supported signaling checkout readiness to distinguish discoverable third-party or customized products from natively purchasable goods. Ilya Grigorik countered that Shopify deprecated an identical boolean because agents misinterpreted `false` to mean that carts or checkouts could not be constructed, when in practice agents can negotiate line items, addresses, and discounts up to the final payment step. James Andersen and Ilya Grigorik agreed that "native" has inconsistent definitions across platforms (in-app webview, embedded merchant protocol, voice agents) and that flat booleans mislead autonomous agents.
  - **Working Group Position:** Exclude the flat `is_native_checkout` boolean from the core specification. Surface availability will be modeled through platform extensions, while the broader policy expectation of catalog items supporting cart capabilities is escalated to the Shopping Tech Council.
  - **Context:** [DWG Charter: Bulk Product Discovery](https://github.com/Universal-Commerce-Protocol/ucp/issues/682)

- **Topic:** Base pull request status and December 10 release timeline
  - **Discussion:** Vinod Kumar asked about blockers on the base pull request (#866). Ilya Grigorik reported pushing an update addressing custom records and committed to marking the pull request ready for review after polishing outstanding items. Greg Smith confirmed that December 10, 2026 is an immovable release deadline with holiday backstops. The group reviewed feedback regarding delta feeds; Ilya Grigorik proposed hoisting delta feeds out into a separate follow-up issue to preserve the base release scope. James Andersen suggested conducting a final review in next week's session before transitioning the pull request to the Shopping Tech Council.
  - **Working Group Position:** Ilya Grigorik will finalize the base pull request (#866) and mark it ready for review. The working group will review and sign off on the pull request during the November 4 session ahead of the December 10 release. Delta feeds and attribute recommendations will be tracked in separate follow-up issues.
  - **Context:** [feat: [DRAFT] feed capability for bulk catalog access #866](https://github.com/Universal-Commerce-Protocol/ucp/pull/866)

- **Topic:** Compliance attributes and messaging tool framework
  - **Discussion:** Alex Jansen reviewed low-coverage compliance attributes, including unit pricing, energy efficiency labeling, Quebec warranty requirements (Bill 29), California Proposition 65 consumer warnings, adult content indicators, and mandatory shipping cost disclosures. Alex Jansen noted that pre-computing unit prices in feeds is challenging when shipping and taxes depend on buyer location. Ilya Grigorik explained that Universal Commerce Protocol's small-core philosophy avoids enumerating bespoke regulatory warnings; instead, the protocol's existing `messages` framework of type `warning` carries an explicit disclosure contract that consuming agents must display to users. Alex Jansen and James Andersen emphasized that regulatory disclosures must appear up-funnel in feed records and product detail pages, not solely at checkout.
  - **Working Group Position:** Compliance disclosures will leverage the existing Universal Commerce Protocol `messages` framework with `warning` types in materialized feed records. Warranty policies will be modeled following return policy extension patterns, and unit pricing calculations will be evaluated for feed-specific relaxations.
  - **Context:** [feat: [DRAFT] feed capability for bulk catalog access #866](https://github.com/Universal-Commerce-Protocol/ucp/pull/866)
