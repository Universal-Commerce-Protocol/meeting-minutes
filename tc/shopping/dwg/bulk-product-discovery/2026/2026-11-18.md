# UCP Bulk Product Discovery Domain Working Group (DWG) Meeting - 2026-11-18

- **Sponsoring DTC:** Shopping Tech Council
- **Charter:** [DWG Charter: Bulk Product Discovery](https://github.com/Universal-Commerce-Protocol/ucp/issues/682)
- **Led by:** Vinod Kumar, James Andersen (co-chairs)

> The group reviewed Universal Commerce Protocol (UCP) Summit feedback on discovery feeds, confirmed that the base feed capability pull request (#866) has addressed remaining comments and secured co-chair and member approvals ahead of final Shopping Tech Council sign-off, and reviewed three follow-on product schema pull requests (#1069, #1070, #1071). Members aligned on reusing existing UCP authentication mechanisms with unguessable capability URLs for third-party feed providers while keeping `.well-known/ucp` strictly on each merchant's authoritative origin, agreed to park delta feeds in favor of supplementary feeds and prioritize a standardized feedback mechanism instead, and watched Tejas Mehta demonstrate a working Shopify storefront feed implementation. Concluding that the initial DWG charter deliverables are substantially complete, the group agreed to skip next week's US holiday and hold a final wrap-up meeting on December 2, 2026 to finalize Shopping Tech Council handoff and explore a future catalog DWG charter.

## 2. Technical Discussions & Working Group Consensus

- **Topic:** UCP Summit discovery feeds recap
  - **Discussion:** James Andersen reported that the UCP Summit featured an approximately one-hour session on discovery feeds with strong engagement from participants across Amazon, Microsoft, Shopify, PayPal, Salesforce, and Stripe. James Andersen noted spending roughly 40 minutes detailing how the common feed capability supports arbitrary record types while the shopping feed extends the Universal Commerce Protocol (UCP) product schema and supplemental feeds handle shorter-interval updates, while Jordan Williams (Amazon) shared challenges Amazon faces consolidating portfolio product data.
  - **Working Group Position:** Noted; summit questions directly informed the authentication clarifications and follow-on schema pull requests reviewed in this session.
  - **Context:** [feat: feed capability for bulk catalog access](https://github.com/Universal-Commerce-Protocol/ucp/pull/866)

- **Topic:** Authentication and third-party feed provider delegation
  - **Discussion:** Following up on a UCP Summit question, Vinod Kumar, James Andersen, Ilya Grigorik, and Reid Kairalla examined authentication when a merchant hosts its UCP profile while a third-party provider such as Feedonomics hosts the feed list or feed files. Ilya Grigorik explained that the capability reuses existing `UCP-Agent` authentication mechanisms (API keys, private clients, or Web Bot Auth signatures) at the feed list endpoint paired with unguessable capability URLs for feed files, discouraging inventing bespoke authentication schemes. Reid Kairalla confirmed Stripe's implementation is well covered by existing UCP authentication, Vinod Kumar asked how third-party providers distinguish per-platform feeds for Google versus Meta, and Alex Jansen asked whether supporting multiple authentication methods is a broader UCP concern (which Ilya Grigorik confirmed, noting businesses and platforms can also negotiate custom out-of-band methods such as mutual TLS (mTLS)).
  - **Working Group Position:** Reuse existing UCP authentication mechanisms and unguessable capability URLs rather than adding new authentication primitives. James Andersen will add a reference setup note to PR [#866](https://github.com/Universal-Commerce-Protocol/ucp/pull/866) clarifying multi-provider scenarios.
  - **Context:** [feat: feed capability for bulk catalog access](https://github.com/Universal-Commerce-Protocol/ucp/pull/866)

- **Topic:** Well-known discovery for aggregators and origin authority
  - **Discussion:** Jason Mathnov noted that many PayPal merchants lack the technical capability to host `.well-known/ucp` profiles and asked whether PayPal could serve well-known files on merchants' behalf using path-based tenancy under a PayPal domain. Ilya Grigorik explained that the well-known Request for Comments (RFC) prohibits redirects and requires hosting directly on the authoritative origin domain because cross-domain origin claims lack safe Domain Name System (DNS) binding machinery; however, aggregators can expose bespoke merchant subdomain origins (for example, per-merchant subdomains recognized by partnering platforms), and merchants can still delegate the feed list endpoint itself to third parties inside their profile.
  - **Working Group Position:** Confirmed that `.well-known/ucp` remains strictly bound to the origin domain; no change to discovery rules.
  - **Context:** [DWG Charter: Bulk Product Discovery](https://github.com/Universal-Commerce-Protocol/ucp/issues/682)

- **Topic:** Base feed capability pull request (#866) review status
  - **Discussion:** Ilya Grigorik reported pushing a quick update just before the meeting to address Alex Jansen's and remaining review feedback on PR [#866](https://github.com/Universal-Commerce-Protocol/ucp/pull/866), which already holds formal approvals from Vinod Kumar, Reid Kairalla, James Andersen, and Alex Jansen. Ilya Grigorik and James Andersen reminded working group members to use GitHub's official review `Approve` action rather than leaving sign-off buried in general comments, and agreed to nudge Amit Handa to complete Shopping Tech Council review this week.
  - **Working Group Position:** PR [#866](https://github.com/Universal-Commerce-Protocol/ucp/pull/866) is ready for final Shopping Tech Council review and merge.
  - **Context:** [feat: feed capability for bulk catalog access](https://github.com/Universal-Commerce-Protocol/ucp/pull/866)

- **Topic:** Product schema pull requests (#1069, #1070, #1071)
  - **Discussion:** James Andersen presented three follow-on pull requests capturing the working group's product schema recommendations: PR [#1069](https://github.com/Universal-Commerce-Protocol/ucp/pull/1069) (adding `Product.brand`, `Variant.mpn` for Manufacturer Part Number, and clarifying `Variant.barcodes` for scannable Global Trade Item Numbers (GTINs)), PR [#1070](https://github.com/Universal-Commerce-Protocol/ucp/pull/1070) (adding `Variant.condition` as an open string with well-known values `new`, `refurbished`, and `used`), and PR [#1071](https://github.com/Universal-Commerce-Protocol/ucp/pull/1071) (adding optional `product_option.key` with well-known keys `color`, `size`, `gender`, `age_group`, `material`, and `pattern`, aligned with structured attributes in PR [#401](https://github.com/Universal-Commerce-Protocol/ucp/pull/401)). Alex Jansen referenced an existing condition issue ([#586](https://github.com/Universal-Commerce-Protocol/ucp/issues/586)), and James Andersen noted PRs #1069 and #1070 build on Sarath Surendranath's earlier pull request ([#595](https://github.com/Universal-Commerce-Protocol/ucp/pull/595)), while price range remains un-drafted.
  - **Working Group Position:** James Andersen will open a tracking GitHub issue linking PRs #1069, #1070, and #1071, and members will review all three pull requests to confirm alignment before Shopping Tech Council consideration.
  - **Context:** [feat(catalog): add product brand and variant mpn, clarify barcodes](https://github.com/Universal-Commerce-Protocol/ucp/pull/1069), [feat(catalog): add variant condition](https://github.com/Universal-Commerce-Protocol/ucp/pull/1070), [feat(catalog): add stable key to product options](https://github.com/Universal-Commerce-Protocol/ucp/pull/1071), [feat(catalog): add brand, condition, and availability/brand filters](https://github.com/Universal-Commerce-Protocol/ucp/pull/595), [[Feat]: Support specifying the condition (e.g., used, refurbished) of a variant to identify not-new items in Catalog search.](https://github.com/Universal-Commerce-Protocol/ucp/issues/586)

- **Topic:** Feed feedback mechanisms versus delta feeds
  - **Discussion:** James Andersen relayed a UCP Summit question on how feed providers detect ingestion failures and policy rejections, noting Ilya Grigorik's suggestion that a feedback mechanism may apply across UCP beyond bulk feeds (such as mid-funnel analytics). Ilya Grigorik distinguished transport-level reporting (such as HTTP Network Error Logging) from asynchronous business-level validation notifications. Alex Jansen and Vinod Kumar noted that Google Merchant Center (GMC) and Meta expose pull-based issue and error-report APIs, Tejas Mehta described Shopify's bespoke resource-feedback push integrations with Google and Meta, Reid Kairalla noted Stripe polls long-running batch feed handles for syndication results, and Zhaocheng Li and Jason Mathnov cited the difficulty of building feedback systems across disparate platform standards and losing click visibility after moving to UCP checkout. On sync strategy, Vinod Kumar summarized the working document comparison showing supplementary feeds accomplish nearly everything delta feeds offer (processing slightly more rows since they diff against the base feed rather than prior deltas) and confirmed Meta's catalog team aligns with supplementary feeds.
  - **Working Group Position:** Consensus to park delta feeds and recommend that UCP prioritize a standardized platform-to-business feedback mechanism as a higher-value follow-up effort outside the initial DWG scope.
  - **Context:** [DWG Charter: Bulk Product Discovery](https://github.com/Universal-Commerce-Protocol/ucp/issues/682)

- **Topic:** Shopify storefront feed implementation demonstration
  - **Discussion:** Tejas Mehta demonstrated a Shopify storefront implementation built against PR [#866](https://github.com/Universal-Commerce-Protocol/ucp/pull/866), running a script that fetches `.well-known/ucp`, verifies unauthenticated `401` gating versus authenticated requests using a Shopify developer token, and downloads a contextual `JSONL` catalog manifest (scoped by market, language, and currency) via an unguessable capability URL covering two products and fifteen variants. Tejas Mehta noted that serializing Shopify's existing contextual feeds required minimal code. Vinod Kumar asked how merchants could delegate feeds to third parties such as Feedonomics for attributes Shopify does not natively model (such as multiple variant-level images); Tejas Mehta and Ilya Grigorik explained that Shopify built the first-party path first and would need to evaluate delegation trust contracts separately, while missing catalog shapes (such as option-level images) belong in shared catalog schema evolution.
  - **Working Group Position:** Demonstration recorded as validation of the PR [#866](https://github.com/Universal-Commerce-Protocol/ucp/pull/866) architecture and input for future catalog schema work.
  - **Context:** [feat: feed capability for bulk catalog access](https://github.com/Universal-Commerce-Protocol/ucp/pull/866)

- **Topic:** Push-based ingestion models versus pull-based discovery
  - **Discussion:** Alex Jansen asked whether UCP should standardize a platform-side push webhook because many merchants actively push updates directly to Google Merchant Center rather than relying solely on pull polling. Reid Kairalla favored real-time product lookup over push ingestion for reducing indexing latency, noting that existing push APIs vary widely across platforms in request shapes, long-running operation handles, asynchronous processing, and ingestion rate limits. Ilya Grigorik noted that while UCP standardizes catalog shapes and polling intervals (and order webhooks provide an architectural precedent), push mechanisms span a spectrum from lightweight sitemap-style update pings to fat delta payloads, and pushing gigabyte-scale payloads introduces substantial asynchronous job complexity without removing the need for real-time catalog, cart, and checkout verification at purchase time.
  - **Working Group Position:** Keep push-based ingestion webhooks out of the current baseline; revisit alongside delta feeds only if a future scope requires them.
  - **Context:** [RFC: A Bulk Product Discovery Layer for UCP](https://github.com/Universal-Commerce-Protocol/ucp/issues/550)

- **Topic:** DWG charter completion, wrap-up schedule, and future catalog scope
  - **Discussion:** Reviewing the original DWG charter checklist (feed transport, wire format, schema data model, feed extensibility, sync mechanisms, access and authentication, source authority, and capability specification), James Andersen and Ilya Grigorik noted that the group has fulfilled its core objectives in PR [#866](https://github.com/Universal-Commerce-Protocol/ucp/pull/866) and the follow-on schema pull requests. Vinod Kumar and Alex Jansen raised additional catalog domains—such as shipping profiles, promotions, offers, fulfillment, and loyalty—which Ilya Grigorik observed span live catalog APIs as well as feeds and therefore warrant a separate Catalog DWG charter rather than extending this group's scope. Given next week's US holiday and member travel, James Andersen proposed skipping next week and reconvening in two weeks on December 2, 2026 for a final wrap-up session.
  - **Working Group Position:** Consensus to declare the initial Bulk Product Discovery DWG charter complete once PR [#866](https://github.com/Universal-Commerce-Protocol/ucp/pull/866) lands, skip the November 25 meeting, and hold a final wrap-up session on December 2, 2026 to recap deliverables, organize Shopping Tech Council feedback, and discuss potential Catalog DWG proposals.
  - **Context:** [DWG Charter: Bulk Product Discovery](https://github.com/Universal-Commerce-Protocol/ucp/issues/682)
