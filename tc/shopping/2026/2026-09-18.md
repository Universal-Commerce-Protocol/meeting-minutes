## 1. General Topics

- **Topic:** UCP Summit Planning and Communication
  - **Notes:** Amit Handa reported that emails carrying a rough agenda and dates would go out to participants by end of day or early the following week. The initial invitation covers Shopping Tech Council maintainers and public contributors; the detailed agenda will follow, with the email limited to dates so attendees can begin planning. Amit Handa and Ilya Grigorik will coordinate on bringing in participants from the other Tech Councils. Greg Smith confirmed the attendee list and guidance sufficient to book flights and hotels would be shared over chat shortly after the meeting, with events carrying fixed start and end times rather than granular agendas.
- **Topic:** Inventory Scarcity and Data Exposure Risks
  - **Notes:** Jing Li and James Andersen raised a roadmap topic from the Bulk Feeds DWG covering exposure of specific inventory quantities to signal scarcity. James Andersen noted a member had flagged missing quantity in the feeds specification, while Greg Smith had previously clarified that fine-grained inventory quantity is intended for checkout rather than public feeds. Ilya Grigorik described the business risk directly: malicious bots run binary searches over checkouts to track sales velocity and leak proprietary data. Amit Handa and Ilya Grigorik agreed the protocol should not prohibit the feature outright but must offer flexibility, letting merchants choose between exact counts, a boolean availability flag, or authenticated contextual feeds for specific channels. Jing Li reiterated interest in exploring scarcity as a conversion lever in the next roadmap.
- **Topic:** Availability Schema and Scarcity Modeling
  - **Notes:** James Andersen proposed adding quantity as an optional field. Ilya Grigorik outlined the layers involved — raw integers, a simple availability boolean, and low-stock enums — and noted that availability currently couples status with sellability, which breaks pre-order and back-order handling. Julia Downey connected this to scarcity incentives such as limited availability on airline flights, and agreed with Ilya Grigorik that availability and scarcity need to be decoupled and revisited. Amit Handa suggested using message constructs for promotional information or reusing info codes for upsells; Ilya Grigorik clarified that messages carry well-defined codes to maintain a concrete contract. Julia Downey raised concern about overloading messages for recommendations, and the group deferred the detailed design.
- **Topic:** Domain Working Group Scope on the Product Data Schema
  - **Notes:** James Andersen asked whether the DWG charter scope extends to the product data schema in order to address items such as saleable item quantity. Ilya Grigorik confirmed that closing identified gaps is acceptable provided the group avoids inventing a parallel product schema and instead handles the distinction between public and authenticated contexts. James Andersen agreed to take the feedback back to the working group.
  - **Decision:** The group aligned on including product data schema additions within the scope of the domain working group.
  - **Context:** [Domain Working Group (DWG) Charter: Bulk Product Discovery](https://github.com/Universal-Commerce-Protocol/.github/issues/53)
- **Topic:** Deep Dive Scheduling and Vertical Council Cadence
  - **Notes:** Jing Li raised a process question on behalf of the Lodging DTC regarding signing up for deep dive slots with Governing Council members to review spec proposals. Amit Handa recommended starting with a lightweight open office hours approach with councils participating as needed. Ilya Grigorik recommended keeping vertical councils on independent cadences with separate slots to avoid scheduling bottlenecks, reserving joint meetings as exceptions. Niranjan Manjunath asked what a fair time expectation is for Governing Council members per vertical. Amit Handa proposed keeping roughly two slots a week open and rotating the audience, and agreed with Jing Li to settle dedicated parallel scheduling offline.
  - **Decision:** The group agreed to reserve a separate, net-new time slot for Lodging office hours with the Governing Council rather than preempting the Monday shopping slot.
- **Topic:** Roadmap Milestones and Implementer Priorities
  - **Notes:** Jing Li asked about the review session and locking down the roadmap. Greg Smith responded that based on feedback he plans to split the document across two milestones, take a first cut, and solicit group feedback. Ilya Grigorik and Amit Handa discussed weighting implementer interest and key drivers, suggesting that items without an active implementer behind them are a signal to deprioritize. Greg Smith noted many tracked items already have previous work and interested owners as a starting point.
  - **Decision:** The roadmap will be split across two milestones to improve forecasting.

## 2. Open Discussions & PR Reviews

- **Topic:** Ask Capability — Authority Boundaries and Side Effects
  - **Notes:** Amit Handa opened the discussion on where to draw the boundary for side effects and authority, noting the tension between agents performing actions directly and maintaining strict typed cart and checkout schemas. Ilya Grigorik asserted that `ask` provides unstructured Q&A verbs and must not subsume other tools such as cart creation, which remain the responsibility of the calling agent. Amit Handa agreed to align with that position.
  - **Context:** [feat: `ask` capability for natural-language Q&A](https://github.com/Universal-Commerce-Protocol/ucp/pull/538)
- **Topic:** Ask Capability — UI Rendering and Latency Handling
  - **Notes:** Amit Handa raised the tension between simplicity and human UI rendering, noting agents quickly demand rich content such as images, video, and product cards, and separately flagged high latency from LLMs in the loop alongside multi-turn conversation tracking. Ilya Grigorik responded that conversation IDs follow the same contract as shopping carts, where merchants control retention duration or opt for single-shot request-responses without a conversation ID.
  - **Context:** [feat: `ask` capability for natural-language Q&A](https://github.com/Universal-Commerce-Protocol/ucp/pull/538)
- **Topic:** Ask Capability — Prompt Injection and Sanitization
  - **Notes:** Amit Handa raised prompt injection, noting Q&A responses could carry malicious instructions or unexpected follow-up questions from an LLM. Ilya Grigorik agreed, drew the parallel to fulfillment descriptions that could smuggle the same content, and emphasized that all external content must be treated as untrusted data and sanitized before an agent acts on it. The group agreed that adding explicit guidance to the specification resolves the concern.
  - **Context:** [feat: `ask` capability for natural-language Q&A](https://github.com/Universal-Commerce-Protocol/ucp/pull/538)
- **Topic:** Ask Capability — Global Identifiers and Opaque Object Handling
  - **Notes:** Amit Handa questioned whether platforms need to understand global identifiers and taxonomy details when a query mixes products, carts, and checkouts. Ilya Grigorik clarified that global identifiers are manufactured by businesses and should be treated as opaque blobs by platforms, and that the schema already allows passing sets of identifiers such as SKUs without introducing a new contract. James Andersen and Amit Handa raised whether platforms must distinguish product IDs from cart IDs on the way back; Ilya Grigorik reiterated that `ask` produces no side effects and platforms only pass back optional structured references they already hold, which prevents reinventing UCP over unstructured text.
  - **Context:** [feat: `ask` capability for natural-language Q&A](https://github.com/Universal-Commerce-Protocol/ucp/pull/538)
- **Topic:** Subscriptions Modeling and Payments Tech Council Integration
  - **Notes:** Amit Handa opened a discussion on whether subscriptions should be modeled as a shopping domain construct alongside orders or handled as a payment construct. Ilya Grigorik noted a previous pull request included an initial sketch covering pay-now versus pay-later, with gaps Daniel Wyckoff had identified. Drew Olson argued subscriptions belong under payments and should be picked up as the Payments Tech Council spins up, while acknowledging non-payment components such as newsletter signups. Jing Li questioned the boundary between the shopping and payments roadmaps; Drew Olson responded that placement will require heuristic judgement case by case. Greg Smith noted shopping initiatives blocked on payments can still proceed with explicit review from Payments TC code owners, a structure Ilya Grigorik confirmed is already established.
- **Topic:** Media Variants
  - **Notes:** Amit Handa reported the media pull request is non-controversial and nearing sign-off, with only small documentation items outstanding, and asked others to take a look before it lands.
  - **Context:** [feat(media): model image, video, and 3D variants](https://github.com/Universal-Commerce-Protocol/ucp/pull/690)
- **Topic:** Return Policy Extension
  - **Notes:** Amit Handa thanked Greg Smith for reviewing the return policy extension and noted most comments are addressed. Ilya Grigorik has internal feedback to triage and offered to pair with Julia Downey to unblock the pull request.
  - **Context:** [feat: add return policy extension (dev.ucp.shopping.policy.return)](https://github.com/Universal-Commerce-Protocol/ucp/pull/634)
- **Topic:** WebMCP Progress
  - **Notes:** James Andersen asked about the status of WebMCP priorities. Ilya Grigorik confirmed a draft is in progress and will be brought to the group the following week, noting that implementing MCP over the web surfaced unexpected quirks and differences from the initial model.
